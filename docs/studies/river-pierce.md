# River Pierce Foundation AR Showcase — Case Study

**Brief.** The River Pierce Foundation commissioned an augmented reality project for its 2025 program at the Treviño–Uribe Rancho. The piece debuted on 10 May 2025 as the closing item of The Teaching of the Hands: a free screening of Carolina Caycedo and David de Rozas' documentary, then a panel with Carrizo Comecrudo representatives, then the AR at 3 pm. The content is specific to the place. Each object in the piece carries a panel that names its role in Carrizo Comecrudo practice, and a second reel sets the piece in San Ygnacio with a stone inscription dated 3 December 1871.

**Process.**

1. The brief was the place. San Ygnacio sits on the Rio Grande, and the rancho is a historic building held by the foundation under a Mellon Foundation Humanities in Place grant.
2. The content is interpretive rather than decorative. The bison skull is described as a symbol of life, sustenance, and respect, and the panel says the animal was not as central to Carrizo daily survival as it was for Plains cultures.
3. The bear carries its own panel: animals are treated as relatives and sources of strength, and bear skins are worn in a Carrizo Comecrudo ceremony called the Bear Dance.
4. The poles are named Xie Kua'k Kua̲m. The panel ties them to the four directions and to ceremonial use by the Carrizo.
5. A peyote cactus sits with a panel that quotes a peyote ceremony account and names Esto'k Gna culture.
6. The second reel sets five purple glyph discs above a bilingual inscription: La paz de Cristo sea con nosotros, 3 December 1871, S. Ygnacio ruega por nosotros.
7. The program fixed the schedule: screening at 1 pm, panel at 2 pm, AR debut at 3 pm, food by Malaquite at 3 pm.
8. The event was free and open to the public, so a visitor with no appointment had to be able to take part.
