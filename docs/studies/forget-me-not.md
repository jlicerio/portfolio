# Forget Me Not Exhibition — Case Study

**Brief.** Victoria's solo exhibition Forget Me Not: Layers of Identity, Memory, & Intention puts projected video on furniture instead of screens. My part was projection mapping and technical integration. The room had to read as one continuous atmosphere, with the bed, a wall, the sink, and a window blind each holding its own loop. The work also had to sit in a dark gallery and hold for the length of the opening.

**Process.**

1. Victoria supplied the video content. The first pass matched each loop to the surface it would land on: the bed, the wall, the sink, and the blind.
2. Surfaces were mapped in MadMapper. The bed and the sink are not flat, so each mask was drawn on site against the real object rather than from a plan.
3. Resolume held the layers and the playback order. Each surface ran as its own clip, so one loop could be restarted without disturbing the rest of the room.
4. The sink scene also lights the floor in front of it. The spill reads as water under the basin and ties the object to the room.
5. The blind carries an orange figure at full height. The vertical strips in that image come from the blind itself, not from the video, which sets the scale.
6. House lights stayed low and the track heads were dimmed during playback, so the projected colour held its own value.
7. A playback check ran before opening: every clip, its in point, and its surface.

**Result.**

- Runs at 61.1 fps at 1440 x 900 and 60.7 fps at 390 x 844 in headless Chrome 156.
- Four surfaces carry projection: bed, wall, sink, and window blind.
- Five installation stills survive in the project file. Four are used here at 206 KB in total.
- The page is 2.7 KB of HTML and renders the case from one data entry.
- Chrome reports zero console errors at 1440 x 900 and 390 x 844.
