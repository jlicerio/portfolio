# Forget-me-not — Case Study

**Brief.** Forget-me-not is Victoria's first solo exhibition. It puts video on household objects instead of screens, and the set covers identity, archive, memory, aging, and home. The show ran at St. Edward's University in February 2025 with five installations: Forget-me-not, Entre Mundos, Sueños Rosas, 'Til Death Do Us Part, and Tender Cuts. My part was projection mapping and technical integration: fit Victoria's video onto the bed, the wall, the sink, and the window blind, and hold each object's image in place through the opening.

**Process.**

1. Victoria's video is the content. Each loop was matched to one surface before anything was mapped.
2. Surfaces were mapped in MadMapper. The bed, the sink, and the basin are not flat, so each mask was drawn on site against the real object rather than from a plan.
3. Resolume held the layers and the playback order. Each surface ran as its own clip, so one loop could restart without touching the rest of the room.
4. The sink scene also lights the floor in front of it. The spill reads as water under the basin and ties the object to the room.
5. The blind carries an orange figure at full height. The vertical strips in that image come from the blind, not from the video, which sets the scale.
6. One work carries projection off the furniture: a cloth kite hangs above the slide, and the slide itself takes colour from the room rather than from a projector.
7. House lights stayed low and the track heads were dimmed during playback, so the projected colour held its own value.
8. A playback check ran before opening: every clip, its in point, and its surface.

**Result.**

- Runs at 60.9 fps at 1440 x 900 and 60.8 fps at 390 x 844 in headless Chrome 156.
- Five installations in the show, with the room mapping a bed, a wall, a sink, a basin, a window blind, and the floor in front of the sink.
- Venue and date: St. Edward's University, February 2025.
